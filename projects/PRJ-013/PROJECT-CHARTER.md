## 1. Project purpose

To develop a rigorous, source-based research and public-dialogue initiative on the constitutional rights, historical experiences, social inclusion and economic participation of Banjara and other Denotified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic communities; to document the proposed **Sahakari Kutumbi** philosophy and community-cooperation model; and to produce a clearly labelled fictional AI-based mock parliamentary session representing the 2026 Parliament.

The project is intended to encourage informed constitutional discussion and invite voluntary participation from MPs, political parties, researchers, legal experts, community organisations, cooperatives and the public.

## 2. Core ideas

1. **Constitutional justice:** equality, dignity, liberty, non-discrimination and effective access to public institutions.
2. **Historical examination:** evidence-based study of colonial-era criminalisation, including the Criminal Tribes Act, 1871, its repeal and the continuing effects of stigma.
3. **Sahakari Kutumbi philosophy:** the project's proposed description of family and community life grounded in mutual support, shared responsibility and voluntary cooperation. It is a social-philosophical proposal, not a compulsory identity or legal status.
4. **Community cooperation:** voluntary, democratic and accountable cooperative institutions that can support livelihoods, education, skills, enterprise and community development.
5. **Democratic pluralism:** political parties and MPs are represented through their documented public positions, with room for disagreement, scrutiny, amendments and independent judgment.

## 3. Main objectives

- Produce a multi-volume, university-oriented research publication.
- Study the constitutional and legal position of Banjara and DNT/NT/SNT communities, recognising that classifications and entitlements vary by state and community.
- Examine public policy, commissions, government schemes and parliamentary records.
- Build a verified research register of the sitting Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha members at a declared 2026 data cut-off.
- Document party ideologies and individual MPs' public positions using reliable, attributable sources.
- Invite MPs to provide their own written views, approved speeches, corrections and policy suggestions.
- Draft a constitutional and legally reviewed community-cooperation policy proposal.
- Create a fictional AI mock parliamentary session with transparent disclosures and consent-based use of likeness, voice and supplied material.
- Publish research, methods, corrections, contributors and project progress openly.

## 4. Proposed publication programme

The working concept is an 18-volume series. The final number, titles and page counts will be fixed after the research inventory and editorial plan are approved.

Proposed subject areas include:
- Indian democracy, constitutional equality and social justice.
- Banjara history and colonial criminalisation.
- Current conditions of Banjara and DNT/NT/SNT communities.
- Constitutional rights, legal classifications and public policy.
- Commissions, reports and government programmes.
- Political party ideologies and documented policy positions.
- Individual MP profiles and parliamentary records.
- Sahakari Kutumbi philosophy.
- GBSBFORYOU community-cooperation model.
- Proposed national policy and legislative framework.
- Mock Parliament agenda, speeches, amendments, debate and fictional proceedings.

The earlier working estimate of approximately 700 MPs is provisional. The final roster must be verified against official Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha records at the stated cut-off date; membership changes, vacancies and nominated members must be handled transparently.

## 5. MP profile and speech standards

Each MP profile may include:
- Name, House, party and constituency/state as officially recorded.
- Publicly documented political and parliamentary background.
- Attributable speeches, questions, committee work and legislative record where available.
- Publicly stated positions relevant to social justice, tribal affairs, DNT/NT/SNT communities and cooperation.
- Source list, publication dates and data cut-off.
- A proposed role in the fictional mock session, clearly marked as a simulation.

**Speech categories must remain separate:**
1. **MP-submitted/MP-approved speech:** supplied or expressly approved by that MP, with permission and provenance recorded.
2. **AI-generated simulated speech:** independently drafted from public records and declared party positions. It must never be represented as an actual statement, endorsement or private view of the MP.

No political position, vote, consent or party endorsement may be invented. Unavailable information must be marked unavailable rather than inferred.

## 6. MP participation and consent

MPs and their offices may be invited to:
- Share written views and policy suggestions.
- Submit or approve a speech for the mock session.
- Suggest amendments to the draft proposal.
- Correct factual errors in their public profile.
- Separately authorise use of their name, likeness, voice or an AI avatar.

Consent for speech publication does not automatically authorise voice or likeness generation. Any use of an identifiable avatar or voice requires specific, documented permission and must be labelled as an AI reconstruction. Non-participation must not be presented as support or opposition.

## 7. Mock Parliament principles

- The production is a fictional educational and research simulation, not an official sitting of Parliament.
- Both Houses and the membership roster will be based on official records at a clearly stated cut-off date.
- MPs and parties will be portrayed neutrally and consistently, based on documented public material.
- Debate may include support, objections, questions and amendments.
- The final simulated vote must follow the debate and recorded positions within the production; unanimity must not be pre-claimed.
- Any fictional passage of a resolution must not be described as a real parliamentary decision, law or government commitment.
- The production must display a clear AI-generated / fictional simulation notice.

## 8. Proposed policy and resolution work

The project may develop a draft **National Community Cooperation and Sahakari Kutumbi Policy Proposal, 2026**, alongside a separate set of recommendations on Banjara and DNT/NT/SNT rights.

Drafting will consider, among other matters:
- Constitutional equality and dignity.
- Voluntary community organisation and cooperative autonomy.
- Education, skills, livelihoods, housing and social protection.
- Reliable data and review of classification-related difficulties.
- Democratic governance, member participation and financial audit.
- Centre–State responsibilities and implementation mechanisms.

All legal claims and proposed legislative language require expert review. A mock-parliament resolution is not itself legislation.

## 10. Governance and editorial safeguards

- Research claims must have traceable sources.
- Distinguish facts, attributed claims, analysis and proposals.
- Apply the same research method to all political parties and MPs.
- Invite community review and record differing views.
- Protect personal data and do not publish private contact details.
- Do not store passwords, API keys, identity documents or sensitive personal data in this public repository.
- Record financial support and expenditure transparently.
- Disclose conflicts of interest, sponsorship and material corrections.
- Do not imply endorsement by Parliament, any MP, political party or public institution without documented authorisation.

## 11. Public contribution and support

The project welcomes voluntary contributions from historians, constitutional and legal experts, researchers, community organisations, cooperative practitioners, technologists, editors, translators and interested citizens.

Contributions may include research references, peer review, translations, accessibility review, documented community experiences and technical support. Financial contributions, if later enabled, will require a separate published budget, receipt and accounting process. A GitHub contribution or public comment does not constitute an MP's or party's endorsement.

## 12. Initial milestones

1. Confirm project charter and editorial principles.
2. Prepare research methodology and source hierarchy.
3. Freeze and verify the 2026 parliamentary roster.
4. Prepare the MP profile template and consent forms.
5. Build the historical, constitutional and policy source register.
6. Consult community members and subject experts.
7. Draft the book architecture and volume plan.
8. Prepare the Sahakari Kutumbi and community-cooperation policy paper.
9. Conduct MP outreach and record responses.
10. Draft and review the fictional mock parliamentary proceedings.
11. Produce and publish the AI simulation with required disclosures.

Dates and budgets for each milestone will be approved after the initial research and resource assessment.

## 13. Success measures

- A traceable, corrected and dated parliamentary profile register.
- Research chapters with reliable citations and peer/community review.
- A clearly defined and legally reviewed cooperation proposal.
- Documented MP invitations, responses and permissions.
- Transparent separation of real MP contributions from AI-generated simulation.
- A published mock parliamentary film with clear fictional/AI disclosure.
- Publicly accessible progress, corrections and financial reporting.
